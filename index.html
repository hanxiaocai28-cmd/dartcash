90-second presentation guide & limitations
  1. Cash needs: show bank records, confirmed bills and the customer's cushion. The $2,000 breakdown is illustrative; the total comes from the model.
  2. Surplus: $5,100 minus $2,000 leaves $3,100. Distinguish an average balance assumption from a real-time transfer authorization.
  3. Options: compare yield with access restrictions. The higher outside offer fails the same-day-access requirement.
  4. Approval: simulate a transfer. Add the upcoming expense; the recommendation is invalidated and needs fresh review.
  5. Economics: try $3,000, $4,000, then $5,100. Customer benefit alone does not imply provider profitability. Reset restores the document baseline.

Not proven: no live AI, bank feeds, transfers, market-rate search or forecast-accuracy evidence. Deterministic rules illustrate the proposed workflow. AI would need to outperform a rules-based baseline on forecasting or less-structured inputs. Shared search costs are not zero servicing costs. Showing outside rates does not remove conflicts of interest.
